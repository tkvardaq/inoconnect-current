Unit Economics

How ROAS & Profitability Are Calculated

Top-line ROAS can be deceptive. A campaign generating a 4.0x ROAS can still lose money if product margins or operating overheads are miscalculated.

01

Realized ROAS Formula

ROAS = Ad Revenue / Ad Spend

Measures gross revenue returned for every dollar spent on paid channels. Example: $75,000 revenue from $20,000 spend yields 3.75x.

02

Break-Even ROAS Hurdle

Break-Even ROAS = 1 / Gross Margin %

The exact return required before your ads produce net contribution margin. At 65% margin, you need at least 1.54x ROAS to break even.

03

Net Ad Profitability

Profit = (Revenue × Margin %) - Spend

Isolates actual contribution dollars into the bank account after deducting product costs (COGS) and media spend.

Expert Knowledge

Frequently Asked Questions

Technical questions regarding ROAS modeling, attribution discrepancy, and profitability metrics.

What is the key difference between ROAS and MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio)?

ROAS measures channel-specific attributed revenue divided by channel ad spend. MER measures total brand revenue divided by total marketing spend across all paid channels. MER provides the ultimate safeguard against double-counting and attribution overlap.

Why does Meta Ads Manager report higher ROAS than Google Analytics 4?

Meta utilizes a 7-day click and 1-day view attribution model, crediting conversions when users viewed an ad even if they later converted via direct navigation or organic search. GA4 utilizes data-driven last-touch attribution, which distributes credit across touchpoints.

How should I calculate my true Break-Even ROAS if I have fixed operational expenses?

To factor in operational overhead, calculate Contribution Margin %: (Revenue - COGS - Payment Fees - Pick & Pack) / Revenue. Your Break-Even ROAS is 1 / Contribution Margin %. For example, if contribution margin is 50%, your true break-even ROAS is 2.0x.

What is a good target ROAS when scaling aggressively?

When scaling aggressively, businesses often operate at 20-30% above break-even ROAS to maximize new customer acquisition volume, relying on 60-day and 90-day repeat purchase cycles (LTV) to generate long-term enterprise value.
