Retention Mathematics

How Email Revenue Compounds

Unlike paid acquisition where cost per click rises with scale, email list economics produce zero marginal media costs for every additional recipient.

01

Campaign Yield Formula

Rev = Subs × Sends × Open% × CTOR% × CR% × AOV

Every email campaign's revenue is the mathematical product of five compounding micro-probabilities.

02

Revenue Per Recipient (RPR)

RPR = Total Email Revenue / Total Emails Sent

The golden yardstick of list health. An RPR of $0.25 to $0.50 indicates strong segmentation and high brand trust.

03

Flow Multiplier Advantage

Flow RPR = Broadcast RPR × 3.2x to 5.0x

Automated behavioral flows sent when intent is peak (abandonment, onboarding) generate up to 5x higher revenue per send than blasts.

Technical Knowledge

Frequently Asked Questions

Deliverability protocols, MPP impact, and list hygiene standards.

How has Apple's Mail Privacy Protection (MPP) affected email analytics?

Apple MPP pre-fetches and caches email images via proxy servers, artificially registering a 100% open rate for iOS users regardless of whether they opened the email. Modern email teams rely on Click-to-Open Rate (CTOR), on-site click attribution, and placed-order events rather than raw open rates.

What percentage of total company revenue should be driven by email marketing?

For healthy e-commerce and subscription companies, email and SMS retention should generate between 25% and 40% of total gross revenue. For B2B businesses, email nurture drives between 30% and 50% of qualified demo bookings and pipeline velocity.

Why is list cleaning and pruning inactive subscribers necessary?

Sending emails to unengaged accounts signals to Gmail, Yahoo, and Outlook that your content is spam, dragging down inbox placement for your most engaged customers. Pruning unengaged subscribers improves overall domain sender score and lowers ESP software tier costs.

What email authentication standards are required in 2025/2026?

Google and Yahoo strictly mandate DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail), SPF (Sender Policy Framework), and a published DMARC policy with a spam rate below 0.3%. Failure to configure these DNS records results in direct delivery to the spam folder.
