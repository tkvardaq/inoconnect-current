AI Marketing Tools:
What to Use, What to Skip and How to Choose

A practical guide to AI marketing tools: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Jasper, Surfer, Clay, Zapier and more, with honest pros, cons and use cases.

Unchecked Hallucinations

Every week there is a new tool promising to write your ads, rank your site and run your outreach while you sleep. Most marketing teams now have a handful of subscriptions, overlapping features and no clear idea which of them is saving time.

This guide covers the AI marketing tools that are worth knowing, what each does well, where each falls short and how to choose by job instead of by hype. It does not quote prices, because those change often. Check each vendor for current plans.

Quick answer

The best AI tools for marketing depend on the job. General assistants like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini handle drafting and analysis. Perplexity suits research. Surfer helps SEO content. Jasper and Canva support brand copy and design. Clay, Zapier and HubSpot Breeze automate outreach and workflows.

How to Choose AI Marketing Tools

Start with the task, not the tool. Ask four questions before you subscribe.

  • What job does it do? Drafting, research, analysis, design, automation or reporting.
  • How much time will it save per week? If you cannot estimate it, run a two-week trial and measure.
  • Does it connect to what you already use? A tool that fits your CRM, CMS and ad platforms is worth more than a standalone one.
  • What happens to your data? Check how the vendor handles customer data, and do not paste confidential or personal information into tools without approval.

General Assistants: ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini

These are the workhorses. You can use any of them for first drafts, headline variations, email sequences, audience research prompts, summarising call transcripts and analysing spreadsheets.

ChatGPT

Pros: broad capabilities, custom GPTs for repeatable tasks, plenty of tutorials and integrations.

Cons: it can produce plausible but incorrect statements, and default output often sounds generic without a style guide.

Use it for: brainstorming angles, drafting outlines, rewriting copy for different audiences.

Claude

Pros: strong with long documents and nuanced writing, useful for analysing reports, transcripts and long briefs.

Cons: fewer built-in marketing integrations than some rivals, so you may need to move files between tools.

Use it for: editing long-form content, summarising research, drafting messaging frameworks.

Gemini

Pros: works inside Google Workspace, which helps if your team lives in Docs, Sheets and Gmail.

Cons: results vary by task, and feature availability depends on your plan and settings.

Use it for: summarising email threads, drafting in Docs, working with Sheets data.

Pick one as your main assistant, and test the others on your three most common tasks. Switching costs are low, so choose on results, not brand loyalty.

Research: Perplexity

Perplexity answers questions with cited sources, which makes it a good starting point for competitor research, market sizing hunches and background reading.

Pros: you can click through to the sources and verify.

Cons: it can cite weak sources or misread them, so check anything you plan to publish or present as fact.

Content and SEO: Jasper and Surfer

Jasper is built for marketing teams that need consistent brand voice across many pieces. Its value is in templates, shared brand settings and team workflows. If you are a solo marketer, a general assistant with a good prompt may be enough.

Surfer analyses pages that rank for a keyword and suggests terms, headings and length. It is useful for briefs and for refreshing existing pages. Do not follow its scores blindly. Content that covers a topic better than competitors beats content that simply matches their word lists.

For the strategy behind this, see our posts on zero-click content and schema markup, which both matter when AI search tools summarise your pages.

Design: Canva Magic Studio

Canva Magic Studio adds AI features such as text-to-image, background editing and layout suggestions to an already easy design tool. It suits social posts, simple ads, slide decks and quick variations for testing.

Limits: generated images need review for accuracy, brand fit and licensing terms, and complex brand work still needs a designer.

CRM, Outreach and Automation: HubSpot Breeze, Clay and Zapier

HubSpot Breeze

Breeze is HubSpot's set of AI features across content, CRM records and customer support. Its edge is context: it can draw on your contact and deal data. If you do not use HubSpot, it is not a reason to switch by itself.

Clay

Clay pulls data from many sources into a spreadsheet-style workspace and lets you add AI steps, such as researching a company and writing a first line. It is powerful for outbound sales and account-based marketing. The limits: it takes time to set up well, and you must follow data protection and anti-spam rules in your markets.

Zapier

Zapier connects apps so that an event in one triggers action in another. Use it to route form leads into your CRM, post alerts to Slack or send AI-generated summaries to a team. Test every automation with sample data, and add a human review step where mistakes would be costly. For deeper builds, see our AI automation service.

AI Tools for Marketing by Business Type

E-commerce

Useful jobs include product description drafts, ad copy variations, review summaries and image edits. Pair a general assistant with Canva for creative variations and Zapier for order and review workflows. Keep product facts, sizing and claims human-checked, since errors cause returns and complaints.

B2B and SaaS

Useful jobs include call transcript summaries, competitor research, case study drafts, outbound research and CRM tasks. Clay and HubSpot Breeze fit here. Example (illustrative): A sales team spends two hours per rep per week researching accounts. If a workflow cuts that by half, a team of six saves about six hours a week, as long as the output is spot-checked. Measure your own numbers.

Healthcare and regulated sectors

Be conservative. Do not enter patient or personal data into general tools. Have a qualified person review any health-related content for accuracy and compliance before it is published.

How to Measure Whether an AI Tool Is Working

  1. Record how long the task took before the tool, using a week of honest timesheets.
  2. Run the tool for two to four weeks on the same task.
  3. Compare hours spent, revision rounds and error rates.
  4. For content, track results in GA4 and Search Console, such as engagement, conversions and rankings, and compare them with comparable pieces made without AI.
  5. For ads, compare click-through rate and conversion rate of AI-assisted variations against your control ads in Google Ads or Meta.
  6. Keep the tool only if the net result, after review time, is positive.

Decision Checklist Before You Buy

  • Can you name the exact task and who does it today?
  • Can a general assistant already do it well enough?
  • Does the tool connect to your CRM, CMS or ad accounts?
  • What data will it see, and is that allowed under your policies?
  • Is there a monthly or annual commitment, and can you cancel easily?
  • Who reviews the output before it reaches customers?

More Questions About AI in Marketing

How do AI search tools affect marketing?

Tools like Perplexity, ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews summarise answers and cite sources. Clear, accurate, well-structured pages with schema and original expertise are more likely to be quoted.

Should I disclose AI use?

Check the rules for your market and platforms, as some require disclosure for certain AI-generated media or ads. Beyond rules, be honest with customers, and never present AI-generated testimonials or fake people as real.

What is the safest first AI project?

Internal tasks with a human check, such as summarising meeting notes, drafting outlines or sorting survey feedback. Mistakes there are cheap and easy to catch, which makes it a good place to build habits.

Common Mistakes with AI Marketing Tools

  • Publishing unedited output. It reads generic, can contain errors and rarely includes original experience.
  • Buying tools before defining jobs. This leads to overlapping subscriptions nobody uses.
  • Sharing sensitive data. Customer details and confidential plans should not go into tools without clear policies.
  • Trusting facts and numbers. Always verify statistics, quotes and claims.
  • Automating a broken process. If your lead follow-up is unclear, automating it just makes mistakes faster.
  • Ignoring brand voice. Without examples and rules, everything sounds the same as your competitors.
  • Not measuring. Track hours saved, output quality and results, not just usage.

Step-by-Step Action Plan

  1. List your ten most repetitive marketing tasks and estimate weekly hours for each.
  2. Rank them by time spent and by risk if a mistake slips through.
  3. Pick the top two low-risk tasks, such as meeting summaries or first-draft outlines.
  4. Choose one general assistant and test it for two weeks with a written prompt template.
  5. Write a short policy: what data is allowed, what needs review and who approves publishing.
  6. Add a specialist tool only when a specific job justifies it.
  7. Measure time saved and quality changes, then keep, adjust or cancel.
  8. Review your stack every quarter, as tools change fast.

AI Marketing Tools FAQ

What are the best AI tools for marketing?

There is no single best. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini suit drafting and analysis, Perplexity suits research, Surfer helps SEO, Canva handles design, and Zapier, Clay and HubSpot Breeze handle automation and CRM tasks.

Can AI replace a marketing team?

No. AI speeds up drafts, research and repetitive work, but strategy, judgement, brand decisions and accountability still need people. Teams get the best results when AI handles first passes and humans review.

Is AI-generated content bad for SEO?

Search engines focus on whether content is helpful, not on how it was produced. Unedited, generic or inaccurate AI content performs poorly. Useful, accurate, original content with expert input can perform well.

How do I start using AI in marketing safely?

Begin with low-risk tasks, avoid pasting confidential or personal data, verify facts, and set a review step before anything is published. Document these rules so everyone follows them.

Do I need paid AI tools?

Not always. Many teams start with one general assistant and add specialist tools only when a task justifies it. Check each vendor's current plans, as features and limits change.

Conclusion

The best AI marketing tools are the ones that remove a specific chore from your week without lowering quality. Start with one assistant, define the jobs, keep a person responsible for the result and add specialists as the need becomes clear.
